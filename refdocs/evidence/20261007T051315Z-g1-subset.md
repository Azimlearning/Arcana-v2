## The pre-registered gate, as it came out

| Clause | Result |
|---|---|
| Concept coverage of the slice's chunks at least 0.8 times the baseline's | **Passed**: 98.3 percent against 99.0 percent |
| Share of node-to-chunk links owned by the largest 1 percent of nodes not above the baseline's | **Failed by 0.6 points**: 14.75 percent against 14.19 percent |
| No large retrieval drop (mean paired nDCG@10 difference at least -0.05, hybrid and graph arms) | **Passed**: hybrid +0.0096, graph +0.0048 |
| Graph arm valid (returns something for at least half of the questions) | **Valid**: empty for 0 of 19 |
| **Ready for Azim to decide** | **No**, because one structural clause failed |

The failed clause is a margin of 0.0056, roughly four percent of the baseline's value. The gate is reported as registered and is not reinterpreted; under D-34 a failed gate is a reason to improve the design and measure again, not to drop the direction.

These differences are tiny and the counts of better and worse are about even: **the atomic graph did not hurt retrieval and did not clearly help it.** The first graph-versus-flat comparison (P1) was already inconclusive and this one cannot settle it.

## What the audit sample and the pilot show qualitatively

Nine edges read from each graph in the blind sample: the baseline's are often noise (reference-list lines read as relations: "H. Ji authored Retrieval-augmented generative question answering..."; an example question read as a fact: "age requirement applies to U.S. Senate"; "C predicts ISUSE" from an algorithm box). The atomic graph's are mostly sensible and evidenced ("WebGPT uses GPT-3", "Q&A pipeline uses LangChain", abbreviation links), with some weak or odd ones (an evidence sentence that is a page header; "other" relations). This is a reading by the assistant and is not a score; the full 100-item sample is for Azim.
