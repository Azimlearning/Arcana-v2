## What this look is, and what it cannot show

The question (Azim): is the graph more valuable when the knowledge base is large? The corpus goes from 285 passages (Looks 1 and 2) to 1,570. The graph here is the **free-form** one: it has no kinds and no evidence sentences, so the vault shows no stated links on pages, people are not excluded from co-mentions, and the asserted-link evidence path is inert (declared in A5). The vault's resolver reads the same node ids as the frozen one, so gate clause 3 is equal by construction and carries no information.

## The registered gate

All four clauses pass (coverage 100 percent, 88.9 percent of pages linked, question words recognised 0.657 equal to the baseline's, nDCG drop -0.033 for the hybrid and +0.011 for the graph arm); all four vault arms valid. Clause 3 and clause 2 are not informative here (see above); clause 4 is the one that is.

## The finding about scale: the frozen graph signal helps the hybrid more as the corpus grows

| Corpus | `flat` (dense + BM25) nDCG@10 | frozen `hybrid` (adds the graph signal) | hybrid minus flat, paired | better / tied / worse |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 papers, 285 passages (Mark 1 slice) | 0.3826 | 0.3611 | -0.0215 | 5 / 6 / 8 |
| 16 papers, 1,570 passages (pinned) | 0.1686 | **0.2080** | **+0.0394** | 7 / 8 / 4 |

(Recall@10, the same comparison: -0.0165 on the slice (2 / 13 / 4), **+0.0268** at full scale (4 / 14 / 1).) On the small corpus adding the graph signal to dense and BM25 did not help and slightly hurt; on the full corpus it helps. This is the direction Azim expected. It is **descriptive**: the same 19 questions and one ingestion on both, a different mix of passages in each (the slice keeps only three papers' passages), and the difference is a few hundredths of nDCG with a handful of questions moving. It supports the hypothesis and does not prove it.

## What the vault did at scale (free-form graph, vault-1)

| Pair | nDCG@10 | better / tied / worse | Recall@10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `vault` against the frozen `graph` | 0.1133 to 0.1244 (+0.0111) | 8 / 5 / 6 | 0.1690 to 0.1378 (-0.0312) |
| `hybrid_vault` against the frozen `hybrid` | 0.2080 to 0.1749 (-0.0331) | 4 / 8 / 7 | 0.2123 to 0.2048 (-0.0075) |

Over the free-form graph the vault is **not** the large gain it was over the g1-3 graph (Look 2: +0.086, 8 better and 1 worse): here it is level with the frozen graph signal, and the hybrid with the vault is a little below the frozen hybrid. Exploratory ablations: the spread discount is again what keeps the vault arm from falling below the frozen graph (without it -0.0067 against the frozen graph and hybrid -0.0394); co-mentions add very little (+0.0024 on the graph arm, -0.0012 on the hybrid); the semantic step helped both slightly (graph arm 0.1244 to 0.1323, hybrid 0.1749 to 0.1846), the opposite sign to Look 2 and equally small. Claims are inert (none).

## Structure at scale

11,052 pages from 11,173 nodes (55 with aliases); 13,156 asserted and 27,403 co-mention links; 88.9 percent of pages linked (79.7 percent by an asserted link, 59.1 percent by a co-mention); every one of the 1,570 passages mentioned by a page. People from title pages are in the vault as ordinary pages (the free-form graph does not say who is a person), so some co-mention links are author-list noise; the 6-page cap removes the longest author lines only.

## Reading it together with Look 2

- The **graph signal gains value with corpus size** (hybrid beats flat at 1,570 passages and not at 285).
- The **vault's own gain depends on the graph it is built over**: large over the evidence-checked atomic graph on the small slice (Look 2), none over the free-form graph on the large corpus (Look 3). The two are different graphs and different corpora, so the difference cannot be attributed to either alone.
- So the next measurement that matters is the **atomic graph at full scale** (the `atomic2` builder over all 16 papers, about $4 to $6, needing the cap raised or the 2026-11-01 reset), which would test both claims at once: does the evidence-checked graph plus the vault beat the free-form graph when the corpus is large?

## Limits

19 questions, one ingestion, descriptive only; the free-form graph has no evidence sentences; the slice and the full corpus differ in more than size; the ablations and semantic arms are exploratory; the vault-1 constants were set on Mark 1's slice (a priori weights, disclosed in D-35).
