## The defect the pilot found

With builder g1-2, **114 of about 226 proposed concepts were dropped as "not verbatim"** and, because relations need both ends, 74 relations then lost an endpoint: 66 concepts and 13 relations survived (the free-form graph for the same paper has 303 nodes and 296 edges). Four real replies compared with their chunks showed why: PDF text breaks words across lines ("an- swers", "prompt- ing", "retrieval- augmented") and the model repairs them when it copies, so an honest copy failed a character-level match. Two further causes are real and stay as drops: the model removing a footnote digit ("Ragas1") and removing a parenthetical citation.

**Amendment (before any measured run), builder g1-3:** a copy is matched to the chunk blind to hyphens and to the space a PDF leaves after one, and what is stored is the chunk's own words (so the stored sentence is still a verbatim span of the stored chunk); labels are matched the same way. The structure measures (`eval/graph_quality.py`) use the same hyphen-blind test for both graphs. On the four chunks the fix took verbatim notes from about 6 of 24 to 22 of 24.

Drops after the fix: 27 notes without the label, 16 not verbatim, 25 evidence sentences without a label, 10 not one sentence, 35 relations whose endpoint was dropped. The atomic graph is much sparser (a tenth of the edges), as a sentence-checking builder must be; whether that costs retrieval is what the slice run measures.

## A second amendment from the pilot: the degree-share clause leaves the gate

The registered gate required the share of edge ends owned by the ten largest nodes not to exceed the baseline's. The pilot shows this rises in **any** graph with fewer edges (41.9 against 24.5 percent here, for sparsity alone), so the clause would fail a sparse graph for a reason that has nothing to do with hubs. It is now reported, not gating; the retrieval-relevant concentration (the share of node-to-chunk links owned by the top one percent of nodes) stays in the gate.

## Limits

One paper, one build, one sample (no temperature is set). The numbers describe the builder's behaviour on a short paper and are not evidence about retrieval.
